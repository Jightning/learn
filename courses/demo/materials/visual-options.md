# Block visual options

This section is a presentation fixture, awaiting the owner's choice. Its three
subsections deliberately repeat the same lesson so visual comparisons do not
depend on different content. It is not a prescribed lesson sequence.

The calculation is independently checkable: a vector with components (3, 4)
has squared magnitude 3² + 4² = 25, hence magnitude 5. The two-leg route has
length 3 + 4 = 7. Changing both component signs preserves the squared magnitude.
The supplied SVG depicts exactly these legs and diagonal.

The options test open typography, bounded panels, and a margin rail. Semantic
accents stay fixed when the course hue changes. Words and structure carry
meaning alongside color; important equations have their own space without
separating them from the explanation they support.

Research informs these design hypotheses, not a ranking of their learning
effects. The IES practice guide supports connecting representations and
alternating worked examples with problem solving:
https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1
The signaling literature supports directing attention to relevant structure:
https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108894333.022
W3C requires a visible alternative to meaning conveyed by color:
https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color

Before general adoption, compare finding information, unaided explanation,
fresh problem solving, and delayed recall. Visual preference alone cannot
establish a retention improvement.
